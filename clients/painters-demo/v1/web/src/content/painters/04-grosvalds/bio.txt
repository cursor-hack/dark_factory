A modernist who fought as a Latvian rifleman in the First World War. Grosvalds's brittle, cubist-tinged figures — soldiers in the trenches, refugees on the road, the long Mesopotamian afternoons of his diplomatic posting — are among the most haunting documents of the war from a Baltic perspective. He died of typhus in 1920 at twenty-eight, leaving an oeuvre of barely a few hundred works and a question about what a longer life would have produced.
