An eccentric expressionist whose self-mythologising — the black cape, the cane, the waxed moustache — was inseparable from the work. Padegs drew jazz musicians, soldiers, prostitutes and dandies of interwar Riga in nervous ink and gouache, refusing the cleanness of his teachers. He staged provocations, painted Madonnas with machine guns, and walked the city as an exhibit of himself. He died of tuberculosis in 1940 at twenty-eight, just before the country he had been drawing was occupied.
