Audience

Who this is for

Enterprises under cost pressure

Large engineering organisations need the same throughput with leaner teams—or the same team covering more product surface—without losing traceability from request to release. The gap is not ambition; it is repeatable execution at sustainable cost.

Founders without a technical co-pilot

Small teams have the idea and the urgency, but not a dedicated engineer to turn specs into shippable software. They need a path from plain-language intent to reviewable output—without hiring a full stack role on day one.

Product

What Dark Factory is

A factory that sits between your issue tracker and your product: you record what you want in the tools you already use; the factory turns that into structured plans, reviewable implementation, and shipped experiences—with status flowing back to the same place your stakeholders already look.

Three outcomes, one factory

  • Execution lane: from a ticket or task to review-ready change in your repository—without manual glue for every issue.
  • Planning lane: from rough requirements to a validated backlog structure you can approve before anything is created in bulk.
  • Product lane: from “ship this experience” to a live customer-facing build—the factory handles the mechanical release path.

Flow

How it works: the factory in the middle

You start from the same ticket or brief your organisation already uses. The factory is the single place where intent becomes plans, code, and releases—so we do not walk the jury through our internal build steps or toolchain on this slide. What matters on stage is the contract: clear inputs, reviewable outputs, and a closed loop back to stakeholders.

  • Transparency where it counts: every run leaves artefacts your team can inspect—not a black-box chat log.
  • Two speeds: implementation when you want code in flight, or answer-only when you need a crisp reply without touching the product surface.
  • Closure: when you accept the change, the tracker reflects Done—the loop completes without extra ceremony.

Flow from tracker through Dark Factory to deliverables and back

Your tracker sends a request into Dark Factory. The factory produces reviewable deliverables in your repository and product. Status and links return to the tracker when work completes or when you merge.

Planning lane

From messy requirements to an approved structure

The factory ingests what is already written on the issue—notes, acceptance hints, scope—and produces a structured plan your leads can inspect. Nothing bulk-writes to your tracker until you are comfortable: you get a dry run, then an explicit approval gate before creation.

  1. Capture requirements from the ticket in one place.
  2. The factory proposes epics, tasks, and subtasks in a validated format.
  3. Review the preview; approve when it matches your intent.
  4. Apply once—re-runs stay safe so you do not spam duplicate work items.
  5. Stakeholders see a concise summary on the same issue they started from.

Under the hood the factory enforces schema checks and idempotent apply; the jury story is governance and cost control, not our file layout.

Shipping lane

Customer-facing product without a release playbook debate

When the user-facing experience is ready, the factory runs the release path—producing a production-ready build and publishing it to managed hosting your team already trusts. The trigger can be the same tracker button your PM uses for scope, so “ship it” is not a separate ritual.

We deliberately do not detail our compile, bundle, or CI graph here: for the jury, the punch line is repeatable delivery from approved mainline to live URL—with confirmation back on the ticket.

Trust

Why this is credible

  • Continuity across factory runs so large items do not reset context every night.
  • Validated plans before anything bulk-lands in your tracker.
  • Explicit approval for high-impact actions—not silent autopilot.
  • Implementation vs answer modes so “just explain this” never accidentally ships code.
  • Merge closes the loop—status returns to Done without manual ticket hygiene.
  • No credentials on slides—access stays in your secure automation layer.

Scope

What we did not claim

Dark Factory is execution leverage, not a substitute for judgment: architecture, security sign-off, and roadmap calls stay human-owned. The factory removes repetitive translation from intent to artefact—especially valuable when headcount is tight or technical depth is missing on the founding team.
